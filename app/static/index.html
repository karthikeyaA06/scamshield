    Why this matters

    In 2025, people in India reported losing ₹19,813 crore to cyber fraud. Karnataka alone lost ₹2,413 crore, the second highest of any state.

    Fake app files and fake payment QR codes need no skill from the scammer, and most of the money is never recovered. Checking before installing or paying is what saves it. Source: I4C data, via IANS

    How a check works

    1. Read, never run

      ScamShield opens the APK as a ZIP archive and reads its permissions, signing certificate and code strings. For a QR code it decodes the payment link. Nothing is installed or executed.

    2. Score with open rules

      Every finding becomes numbered evidence. Published rules give it a score from 0 to 100, with extra weight for dangerous combinations like reading SMS plus a Telegram bot link.

    3. Gemma explains, rules guard

      Gemma 4 E4B, an open-weight model running on this laptop, works out what the combination means and explains it simply. Each reason must cite real evidence, and the model can raise the risk level but never lower it.

    What ScamShield can't do yet

    • Apps that hide their code with advanced packing can slip past a static check. ScamShield flags known packers, but it is a first line of defence, not an antivirus.
    • The real owner of a UPI ID is only known to banks. The name lookup here uses demo data, so always read the name your payment app shows before entering your PIN.
    • Kannada explanations come from a small model and fixed templates. They are clear, but a native speaker should review them before wider use.
